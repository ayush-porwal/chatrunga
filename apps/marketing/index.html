Understand the moves you play

Chaturanga is a desktop app for studying your own chess games. It checks every move with Stockfish, estimates how players at your rating would have played with Maia, and explains the moments that mattered in plain language.

A review of every move

Open a PGN or a saved game and start the review. Stockfish checks each position and grades every move, from best to blunder. The summary shows your accuracy, your average loss and how many inaccuracies, mistakes and blunders you made, and the evaluation graph shows where the game turned.

A coach beside the board

Open a move and Chaturanga explains it: what the move was trying to do, why it works or fails, and what the stronger move achieves.

10…cxb5

The recapture comes with check

Taking the knight on b5 is the move nearly everyone plays here: it is the top human choice at every level from 1100 to 1900. The cost is what comes next. Bxb5+ recaptures with check and uncovers the queen on b3 against f7, while your knight on b8 and bishop on f8 are still at home. Qb4+ was the calmer answer: it checks the king, hits the knight on b5 and offers a queen trade that takes the sting out of White's attack.

The coach writes from what the engines found and from facts read off the board, such as loose pieces, checks and material, so it talks about squares and plans rather than numbers. It adjusts to the rating you review at.

Every move in the text is a link. Click one and the line plays out on the board as a variation, with the move tree and engine lines following along.

Where the words come from

AI commentary uses your own OpenRouter key and the model you choose. The key is encrypted with your system keychain and is only ever sent to OpenRouter. A request is made only when you open a move.

The explanations need a key. Without one, the review still grades every move and shows the engine lines, but no commentary is written.

Analyse any position

The analysis board keeps the engine running and shows several candidate lines with their depth and score. Play moves on the board to branch off; the move tree keeps every variation.

Games come in and go out as PGN, and the games you import are kept in your library.

Play against an engine

Choose any installed engine, pick a side and a time control, from bullet to rapid or one of your own, or play without a clock. Offer a draw or resign when you have seen enough.

Puzzles from the Lichess database

Download the Lichess puzzle database once (almost six million puzzles, free to use under CC0) and train offline. Filter by rating, popularity, theme, solution length, opening and side to move.

Databases

The Databases page also offers a set of analysed positions with best moves and difficulty. Datasets download only when you ask and are stored on your Mac.

Engines, managed or your own

Chaturanga can download Stockfish, Lc0 and the five Maia networks for you, or you can point it at any UCI engine you already have. It tells you when a newer engine release is out and updates only when you ask. You also choose how many CPU threads and how much memory the evaluation engine may use.

Your board, your way

Ten board themes or your own square colours, nine piece sets in four looks, and move sounds with a volume control. Focus board puts the move and engine panels away when you just want to look at the position.

Your games stay on your Mac

Games, reviews, puzzles and engines live on your computer, and there is no account to create. Chaturanga goes online only for:

  • Engine and database downloads, when you start them.
  • Checking GitHub for new Stockfish and Lc0 releases. Updates install only when you ask.
  • AI commentary, if you add an OpenRouter key. Each request carries the facts for one move: the position, what the engines found and the game details.
