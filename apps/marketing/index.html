Your chess games, reviewed and explained

Chaturanga is a free desktop app for studying the games you play. Stockfish grades every move, Maia shows what players at your rating would have played, and an AI coach explains the moments that mattered in plain language.

The coach writes from what the engines found and from facts read off the board, such as loose pieces, checks and material, so it talks about squares and plans rather than numbers. It adjusts to the rating and side you review.

Commentary comes from the AI model you choose on OpenRouter, with your own API key. The key is stored encrypted with your system keychain, and a request is made only for the move you open. Without a key, the review still grades every move and shows the engine lines.

Every move, graded

Open a PGN or a saved game and start the review. Stockfish checks each position and grades every move, from best to blunder. You get your accuracy, your average loss and a count of inaccuracies, mistakes and blunders, and the evaluation graph shows where the game turned.

What players at your rating would play

Maia networks, trained on human games, estimate how often players rated 1100 to 1900 would choose each move. A move most players at your level find is a different lesson from one almost nobody sees, and the coach takes that into account.

Analyse any position

The analysis board keeps the engine running and shows several candidate lines with their depth and score. Play moves to branch off and the move tree keeps every variation. Games come in and go out as PGN.

Stockfish and the Maia networks download from their official releases (Lc0 too on Windows; on macOS and Linux you install Lc0 yourself), or point Chaturanga at any UCI engine you already have.

Play an engine on the clock

Pick any installed engine, a side and a time control, from bullet to rapid or one of your own, or play without a clock. Offer a draw or resign when you have seen enough, then open the review.

Puzzles from the Lichess database

Download the Lichess puzzle database once, almost six million puzzles free to use under CC0, and solve them offline. Filter by rating, popularity, theme, solution length, opening and side to move.

Just the board, when you want it

Ten board themes or your own square colours, nine piece sets in four looks, and move sounds with a volume control. Press F, or choose Focus board in the sidebar, to put the panels away on any board.

Your games stay on your computer

Games, reviews, puzzles and engines live on your machine, and there is no account to create. Chaturanga goes online only for these:

  • Engine and database downloads

    Engines from their official GitHub releases, puzzles from the Lichess database.

    When you start them

  • Release checks on GitHub

    To see whether newer versions of your engines and of the app are out. Engines update only when you ask.

    Automatic

  • AI commentary through OpenRouter

    Your key is sent only to OpenRouter. Each request carries the facts for one move: the position, what the engines found and the game details.

    Only with your key

  • Lichess, if you connect your account

    Sign in on lichess.org to play there from the app and bring your games in. Nothing is sent to Lichess until you connect.

    Only if you connect

  • Anonymous usage data, if you turn it on

    Counts such as reviews run and explanations viewed, with a random id for your installation. Never your games, names, files or keys. Off unless you enable it in Settings.

    Only if you opt in

Opening it the first time

Early builds are not signed with a paid developer certificate yet, so your system asks once before the first launch.

macOS
Drag Chaturanga to Applications. If macOS says it can't check the app, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway (on older versions, right-click the app and choose Open).
Windows
If SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.
Linux
Make the AppImage executable, with chmod +x Chaturanga-*.AppImage or your file manager's "Allow executing as program", then open it.
